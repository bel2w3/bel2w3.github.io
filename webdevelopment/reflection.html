REFLECTION

"Seasons"

Creating my capstone film required me to look deep into my life and ponder my experience with anxiety, my family, honesty and restraint, and how those topics relate to my new life in faith. Translating that into fictional characters and a narrative structure that differed from my own personal life was both very creative and very challenging at the same time. I found myself creating something I am both extremely proud of and battling a lot of unspoken emotions about my life in the process. I was able to give myself a second chance at script writing which in turn, was a learning process for me. I was able to correct a lot of errors I made in my Field Production I course my junior year. While as I’m writing this, the capstone film is not done, I am confident that it is reshaping my understanding of storytelling and what it means to relate to a story.

"Timed Poison"

“Timed Poison” was completed during my Cinematography II course and pushed me to think about a different genre of film that is not easily replicated in American culture. I was also challenged to think critically about form, discipline, and limitations early on which helped me engage more with the story the director was wanting to tell in the Romanian New Wave genre. This stripped the storytelling down to its essentials, making each shot very important. As the production teams script supervisor and producer, I was required to serve as the person syncing the footage on set; writing down the scene number and take number, and making sure the text is readable for the editor in post-production. These roles helped strengthen my understanding of how organization, continuity, and visual consistency shape a film’s effectiveness. This also reinforced the importance of intention behind every creative choice and helped me grow more confident in managing the logistics and stylistic demands of a production, even on a smaller scale.

"Circles"

My Field Production I film called Circles marked a foundational moment in my filmmaking degree, as it was my first time overseeing each stage of the filmmaking process. Writing my first script and directing actors taught me how challenging yet rewarding collaboration can be, especially when learning how to give clear directions to different personality types and give them in a respectable manner. Creating a script I believe was the most challenging part because of the way I learn visually. It was very challenging for me to not write too much and give too much time to the paper, when it could be displayed on screen instead.
